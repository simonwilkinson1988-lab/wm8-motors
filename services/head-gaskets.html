When a head gasket fails

A blown head gasket is one of the most common catastrophic engine failures we deal with. The gasket seals the cylinder head to the block, keeping combustion gases, coolant, and oil in their respective channels. When it fails, these mix — and the consequences range from overheating to total engine destruction if ignored.

Symptoms of head gasket failure

  • Persistent overheating or temperature fluctuation
  • White smoke from the exhaust (coolant burning in combustion)
  • Mayonnaise-like emulsion under the oil filler cap
  • Unexplained coolant loss with no visible leaks
  • Bubbling or pressurisation in the coolant expansion tank
  • Oil contamination in the coolant (oily residue in header tank)
  • Misfires or rough running, particularly when cold

What the repair involves

A head gasket repair done properly is not a quick job. It requires methodical strip-down, inspection, and reassembly to precise torque specifications:

  • Strip ancillaries and remove the cylinder head
  • Inspect head and block mating surfaces for warpage
  • Send head to machine shop for skimming (surface grinding)
  • Pressure test the head for cracks
  • Reassemble with new multi-layer steel gasket set
  • New head bolts (stretch bolts are single-use)
  • New thermostat and coolant temperature sensor where applicable
  • Full coolant system flush and refill with correct spec antifreeze
  • Bleed cooling system and verify no air locks

Common questions

About head gasket repair in Skelmersdale.

How do I know my head gasket has gone?

The classic indicators are white smoke from the exhaust, overheating, coolant loss without visible leaks, and a creamy emulsion under the oil cap. We can confirm with a combustion gas test (sniff test) which detects exhaust gases in the coolant — it's definitive and takes five minutes.

Is it worth repairing or should I scrap it?

Depends on the car's value and condition. If the rest of the vehicle is sound, a head gasket repair at £450-£900 is almost always cheaper than replacing the car. If the engine has other underlying issues (bore wear, cracked block), we'll tell you straight — no point throwing money at a terminal patient.

Do you skim the head?

Always. We send every head to a machine shop for surface grinding. Even if it looks flat to the eye, microscopic warpage from overheating will cause the new gasket to fail. It's a non-negotiable part of doing the job properly.

How long does the repair take?

Typically 1-2 days including the machine shop skim. Some engines with limited access or additional complications may take longer. We'll give you an accurate timescale when we quote based on your specific vehicle.
