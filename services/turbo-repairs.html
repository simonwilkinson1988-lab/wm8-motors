Turbo Repair & Replacement in Skelmersdale

Turbocharger diagnosis, repair, and replacement. Recon or new OE units fitted with full oil feed system inspection. We identify and fix the root cause, not just the symptom.

When turbos fail

Modern turbocharged engines rely on precise oil supply and clean exhaust gas to keep the turbo spinning at up to 150,000 RPM. When oil supply is restricted, contaminated, or the turbo ingests debris, failure is inevitable. Fitting a replacement without addressing the underlying cause guarantees a repeat failure.

Symptoms of turbo failure

  • Whining or siren-like noise increasing with engine speed
  • Blue or black smoke from the exhaust under acceleration
  • Significant loss of power or boost
  • Excessive oil consumption with no external leaks
  • Check engine light with boost-related fault codes
  • Oil in the intercooler pipework
  • Turbo actuator clicking or sticking

Repair vs replacement

Not every turbo issue means a full replacement. Actuator faults, wastegate problems, and minor shaft play can sometimes be addressed without removing the unit. However, when bearing failure, compressor wheel damage, or significant shaft play is present, replacement is the only viable option.

We offer both recon and new OE units depending on budget and availability:

  • Recon units — professionally rebuilt with new bearings, seals, and balanced core assembly. Suitable for most applications and significantly cheaper than new
  • New OE units — factory-fresh from Garrett, BorgWarner, IHI, or Mitsubishi (MHI). Preferred for high-mileage daily drivers where longevity matters
  • Actuator replacement — electronic or vacuum actuators replaced and calibrated without removing the turbo where possible

We check the root cause

Fitting a turbo without checking why the old one failed is negligent. We inspect:

  • Oil feed pipe — restricted or blocked (carbonised oil, mesh filter blockage)
  • Oil return line — kinked, blocked, or leaking
  • Intercooler and pipework — oil contamination indicating seal failure
  • Exhaust manifold — catalyst debris that damages the turbine wheel
  • Air filter and intake — foreign object ingestion
  • Oil condition and service history — poor maintenance is the number one killer

How much does it cost?

Turbo replacement in Skelmersdale starts from £450 for a recon unit fitted, with new OE units from £700+ depending on the application. Variable-geometry turbos and twin-scroll designs are at the higher end. We provide a fixed price based on your specific vehicle and preferred unit type.

Common turbo failures by vehicle

Some vehicles are more prone to turbo issues than others. We regularly deal with:

  • Ford 1.6 TDCi — oil feed pipe carbonisation, actuator failure
  • Vauxhall/Opel 1.7 CDTi — bearing failure from restricted oil feed
  • VW/Audi 1.9 & 2.0 TDI — variable vane sticking, actuator faults
  • Peugeot/Citroen 1.6 HDi — turbo oil seal failure, DPF-related back pressure
  • BMW N47/N57 — wastegate rattle, bearing wear
  • Renault 1.5 dCi — actuator failure, oil feed restriction

Common questions

About turbo repair in Skelmersdale.

Can a turbo be repaired?

Sometimes. Actuator faults and minor wastegate issues can be repaired without replacing the whole unit. However, if the bearings have failed or the wheels are damaged, the turbo needs replacing. We'll assess and advise honestly — we don't replace parts that don't need replacing.

What causes turbo failure?

Oil starvation is the primary cause — usually from blocked oil feed pipes, poor oil quality, or extended service intervals. Foreign object ingestion (from failed catalytic converters or loose intake components) and excessive exhaust back pressure (blocked DPF) are also common culprits.

Recon or new?

Recon units are professionally rebuilt with new bearings and seals, properly balanced, and carry a warranty. They're perfectly suitable for most applications. New OE is preferred if budget allows or if the vehicle is high-mileage and you want maximum longevity. We'll discuss options and let you decide.

Do you check the oil feed?

Always. Fitting a turbo without verifying the oil feed and return is functioning correctly is a guaranteed repeat failure. We inspect and replace the oil feed pipe as standard practice, and check the return line for blockage or restriction.
