Why timing belts matter

Your timing belt keeps the engine's valves and pistons in sync. When it snaps — and they do snap — the valves hit the pistons and you're looking at a new engine or a write-off. It's not a question of if, it's when. Every manufacturer sets a replacement interval (usually 60,000-100,000 miles or 5-7 years) and ignoring it is a gamble with your entire engine.

What we replace

We don't just swap the belt and send you on your way. A timing belt job done properly includes:

  • Timing belt (OE-spec, not pattern)
  • Tensioner bearing and idler pulleys
  • Water pump (it's behind the belt — do it now or pay double labour later)
  • Auxiliary/drive belt if worn
  • Coolant flush and refill
  • Timing marks verified with diagnostic equipment

Timing chains

Some engines use timing chains instead of belts. They're designed to last longer but they do stretch over time, causing timing drift, rattling on startup, and eventually catastrophic failure. Common on VAG 1.4 TSI, BMW N47, Ford 1.0 EcoBoost, and many more. We replace chains, guides, and tensioners as a complete kit.

How do I know if my timing belt needs replacing?

Check your service book for the manufacturer's interval. If you don't know when it was last done, assume it's overdue. Symptoms of a worn belt include ticking noises, engine misfires, or oil leaking from the front of the engine.

Can you do timing belts on my driveway?

Yes — we do timing belt replacements on driveways, car parks, and anywhere with level ground across Skelmersdale and surrounding areas. We bring all the specialist tools needed.

Should I replace the water pump at the same time?

Always. The water pump sits behind the timing belt on most engines. If it fails after a belt change, you're paying the full labour cost again to access it. We include it as standard in our quote.

What brands of belt do you use?

Gates, Dayco, or Continental — OE-equivalent kits that meet or exceed manufacturer specifications. Not cheap eBay pattern kits that fail after 20,000 miles.
